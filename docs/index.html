Built around how Nevada eats

Star ratings won't tell you which Basque hotel still seats strangers at one long table, or which buffet reopened. Nevada Eats is organized the way people here eat: Basque dining, buffets, Chinatown, Hawaiian plate lunch and old Vegas steakhouses, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Clark County places carry the Southern Nevada Health District's own grade card, and Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos. It's an independent app; no health district or county runs it.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a desert highway.

What stays on your phone

Nevada Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Nevada Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The Basque dining, buffet, Chinatown, Hawaiian plate lunch and old Vegas steakhouse lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: its website or menu, a dated news story or tourism post, or its own inspection by the Southern Nevada Health District. The wider directory of 9,573 restaurants starts from Overture Maps' open place data, checked against the health district's permits and Clark County's, Las Vegas's, Henderson's and Reno's business licenses.

Where can I find Basque food in Nevada?

Our Basque list has 4 hand-checked places in 4 towns, from Elko and Winnemucca to Gardnerville and Carson City. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do Las Vegas restaurant grades work?

The Southern Nevada Health District inspects Clark County's restaurants and posts an A, B or C grade card by the door; inspectors take off demerits for each item out of compliance, and an X in the record means the district closed the place. Clark County places show the latest card, its demerits, the date and the items cited, as published. Washoe County, Carson City and the rural counties publish no results as data, so none are shown there.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

Only from a place's own on-premises liquor license in Clark County's, Las Vegas's, Henderson's or Reno's published lists. Resort restaurants usually sell under the resort's license, and other towns publish no list, so a dash means unknown, never no.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
