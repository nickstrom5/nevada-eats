Clark County restaurant grades, explained

The Southern Nevada Health District inspects the restaurants of Clark County, Las Vegas and the Strip included, and posts a grade card where customers can see it: A, B or C. Inspectors take off demerits for each item out of compliance; a B or C card, or a repeat violation, means a follow-up inspection, and a place can earn its A back. When the district closes a place for an imminent health hazard, the record shows an X.

The health district's grade cards

The Southern Nevada Health District publishes every inspection since September 2020 with the grade card it issued and the demerits. For 6,069 Clark County places we list, the app and the web search show the latest card, its demerits and date, earlier inspections and the items cited, in the district's words. The grade is the card the district issued, as published; Nevada Eats never works a grade out from demerits (some B cards carry only a few demerits, because a repeat violation also downgrades). A place the district closed and hadn't re-inspected a month later is left off our lists. One inspection is a snapshot of one day. No page here lists places by grade; the web search can sort the list by it.

Reno, Carson City and the rest of Nevada

Northern Nevada Public Health inspects Washoe County (Reno, Sparks, Incline Village) and posts a placard instead of a letter: green for Pass, yellow for Conditional Pass, red for Failed or Closed. Carson City and the state's rural counties have their own inspectors. None of them publishes results as data, so no result is shown for places there, and no grade is ever computed.

What else Nevada Eats shows

In Clark County, a place's health permit numbers, and its business license where Clark County, Las Vegas or Henderson lists one; in Reno, its city business license. "Serves alcohol" shows only from a place's own on-premises liquor license; anywhere else it's unknown, never no.
